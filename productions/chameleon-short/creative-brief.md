# Chameleon Shorts — actual-footage creative brief

Status: rough cut pending editorial review. No YouTube upload.

## First frame, middle and payoff
The audience sees a green and an orange image of the *same panther chameleon* from a real scientific recording. The next shot actually shows the animal changing from green to yellow and red while the background stays dark. The experiment presented another male chameleon, which triggered the excited color change. The ending returns to that exact green-orange comparison: not a camouflage demonstration but social display.

The source research clips are already **accelerated eight times** by the original authors. Do not describe this as real-time behavior or recolor the animal artificially.

## Timecoded story
- 0–1.85s: before and after are shown side by side; voice: same individual.
- 1.85–5.4s: original color change begins; background is dark, not changing.
- 5.4–11.6s: original scientific motion is shown, with a large closeup + synchronized full-body view.
- 11.6–15.1s: before/after comparison; ask implicitly whether this is camouflage.
- 15.1–18.95s: reveal another male's presence as the experiment's stimulus.
- 18.95–21.2s: closing frame matches opening, visual punchline about standing out.

## Lesson from museum Short
The old movie's TTS said the dogu figurine moved while showing an unrelated static archaeological photo. Here every promised visible change is shown in the *actual video*, not simulated through title-card numbers. CI and audio presence do not make the film creatively approved. Watch the MP4 at phone size, silent, audio-only, and normal and revise if weak.
